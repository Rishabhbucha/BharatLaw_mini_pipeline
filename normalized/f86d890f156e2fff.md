## Ancient Roots of Taxation

The concept of taxation on income and wealth, while seemingly modern, has deep historical roots. The origin of the word "Taxation" is from "Tax," which means an estimate. Evidence from ancient civilizations, including Greece, Germany, and the Roman Empires, shows various forms of levies based on turnover and occupations. Historically, these revenues were used to fund military and civil expenditures and to provide public services. A quote from Kalidas highlights the benevolent nature of taxation:

"It was only for the good of his subjects that he collected taxes from them, just as the Sun draws moisture from the Earth to give it back a thousand fold" – --Kalidas in Raghuvansh eulogizing KING DALIP.

## Kautilya's Arthashastra and Fiscal Policy

Kautilya's Arthashastra, written around 300 B.C., is a seminal text on statecraft and financial administration. Kautilya’s Arthashastra is a complete treatise on ancient Indian polity, economics and military strategy.

Kautilya saw the treasury as the foundation of a government's power. His system included state landlordism, where land revenue (Sita) was a primary income source, complemented by water rates, octroi, tolls, and customs duties. There were also other sources like religious taxes (Bali) and taxes paid in cash (Kara). Vanikpath was the income from roads and traffic paid as tolls.

He laid down detailed laws for foreign trade and the taxes on imported goods. Even gambling operations were centralized and a tax was collected on these operations. A tax called yatravetana was levied on pilgrims.

A major part of the state's revenue came from income taxes, including a proportional tax on actors and musicians, and an excess profits tax. The underlying philosophy was not to exploit citizens but to provide them with protection and welfare services like road construction and education.

Kautilya's system emphasized equity and justice, exempting the poor, sick, and students, and keeping meticulous records of collections and exemptions.

## Modern Direct Taxation Emerges

The concept of direct taxation in India, as we know it today, is a series of these ancient practices. While specific measures have evolved, the core principles of revenue generation for public good and equitable distribution have remained consistent.

This historical foundation laid the groundwork for the more formalized and codified tax laws that would be established in the post-1922 era.

## Taxation in Ancient India

Ancient Indian texts like the Manu Smriti and Arthashastra provide detailed accounts of a well-structured taxation system.

Manu advised that taxes should be proportional to income and expenditure and warned against excessive levies.

He stipulated that traders and artisans should pay 1/5th of their profits, while agriculturists paid a share of their produce (1/6th, 1/8th, or 1/10th) depending on their circumstances. This detailed analysis by Manu on the subject clearly shows the existence of a well-planned taxation system even in ancient times.

Not only this, taxes were also levied on various classes of people like actors, dancers, singers. Taxes were paid in the shape of gold coins, cattle, grains, raw materials, and also by rendering personal service.

Manu also said that the king should arrange the collection of taxes in such a manner that he could promote the industrial and commercial activities of his subjects and that the king should realize taxes from his kingdom, as a bee collects honey from flowers without doing any damage to the flower.

K.B. Sarkar, in his work "Public Finance in Ancient India," praised the system for its productivity and broad base, emphasizing a mix of direct and indirect taxes that covered a diverse population.

#### Preliminary

The Income-tax Act, 1922 was the first step in a new tax regime, formally establishing a specific nomenclature for tax authorities.

The Income-tax Act, 1961 was enacted, replacing the 1922 Act, and came into force from April 1, 1962.

#### Changes in administrative set up since the inception of the department

The Central Board of Revenue was constituted in 1924 as a statutory body to administer the Act.

In 1939, appellate functions were separated from administrative duties, and the first central charge was created in Bombay.

The Central Board of Revenue was bifurcated to form the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) in 1963.

Directorate of Inspection (Income Tax) and the Income-tax Appellate Tribunal were established.

The Directorate of Inspection (Investigation) and the new cadre of Income Tax Inspectors were created.

The Indian Revenue Service (IRS) was formally constituted, and the IRS (Direct Taxes) Staff College was established in Nagpur.

New cadres such as Commissioner (Appeals) and IAC (Assessment) were established.

Recovery functions were transferred from state authorities to departmental officers.

Directorates for Organization & Management Services and Vigilance were created.

Posts of Chief Commissioner (Administration) were created to enhance supervision.

A comprehensive Cadre restructuring was approved to create additional posts and improve tax administration.

- World War II brought unusual profits to businessmen, leading to the introduction of the Excess Profits Tax and Business Profits Tax.

- Special Investigation Branches were set up to handle complex cases.

- Demonetization of high-denomination notes took place.

- A Voluntary Disclosure Scheme was introduced in 1951.

- Internal Audit Scheme and new tax acts like the Estate Duty Act (1953), Wealth Tax Act (1957), and Gift Tax Act (1958) came into force.

- New concepts like the Annuity Deposit Scheme, Functional Scheme, and a new Intelligence Wing were introduced.

- Valuation Cell and a Direct Recruitment process for Class II Income Tax Officers were established.

- The concept of Permanent Account Number (PAN) was introduced.

- The Settlement Commission was created in 1976 for dispute resolution.

- Economic Administrative Reforms Commission was set up.

- The Directorate of Inspection (Systems) was created, beginning the era of computerization with microprocessor-based systems.

- The Direct Tax Law (Amendment) Act, 1987, introduced a uniform previous year.

- The Benami Transactions Prohibition Act was introduced in 1988.

- Presumptive Taxation schemes were introduced to widen the tax base.

- A new PAN series was introduced in 1994.

- The Authority for Advance Rulings was established.

- Significant reduction in tax rates and the introduction of the Voluntary Disclosure Scheme, 1997.

- The Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT) and the "1/6 Scheme" for widening the tax base were introduced.

- The Gift Tax was abolished, and the Kar Vivad Samadhan Scheme was launched.

- Computerized processing of returns was implemented nationwide.

- The National Website of the Income Tax Department was launched.

- The concept of Annual Information Return (AIR), Fringe Benefit Tax (FBT), and Securities Transaction Tax (STT) were introduced.

- E-filing of Income Tax Returns and the Refund Banker Scheme were launched.

- A Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) was set up in Bengaluru for bulk processing of returns.

- The Integrated Taxpayer Data Management System (ITDMS) was launched for a comprehensive taxpayer profile.

- The Direct Taxes Code Bill, 2010 was introduced to simplify the Income-tax Act, 1961.

- CPC-TDS and TRACES (TDS Reconciliation, Accounting and Correction Enabling System) portal was launched.

- A Special Investigation Team (SIT) was formed to investigate black money.

- Equalisation Levy and Country-by-Country Reporting were introduced.

- Launch of the 'E-Proceeding' scheme for electronic assessments.

- Alternate Tax Regime for domestic companies was introduced, and PAN and Aadhaar were made interchangeable.

- The e-Assessment Scheme, 2019 was introduced for a faceless regime.

- Faceless Assessment and Appeal Schemes were introduced, a major step towards digitizing the department.

- Concessional tax rates for individuals were offered, and Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) was abolished.

- The "Vivad se Vishwas scheme" was launched to reduce litigation.

- A new e-filing portal and JSON utility for returns were launched in 2021.

- Exemptions for senior citizens from filing returns in certain situations were provided.

- In 2022, the taxation of Virtual Digital Assets and a tax relief for COVID-19-related compensation were introduced.

- The 'Updated Return' option was introduced, allowing taxpayers to correct their returns even after the due date.

- 2023: The New Tax Regime was made the default option, though the Old Regime remains available. The limit for tax rebate was increased to ₹7 lakh. The tax rates were revised with fewer slabs for a simplified structure.

- 2024: A new form of taxation was introduced for online gaming, and a higher TDS was levied on such winnings. This change aimed to regularize and tax a rapidly growing sector. The government focused on further expanding the tax base through better data analytics and an integrated system with other financial institutions.

- 2025: The Income-tax Act, 2025 has officially been assented to by the President, marking a landmark moment for India's tax reform. This new legislation will replace the six-decade-old Income-tax Act, 1961 , and will come into effect from April 1, 2026 . The 'Updated Return' window was streamlined with a shorter timeframe, encouraging faster compliance.
